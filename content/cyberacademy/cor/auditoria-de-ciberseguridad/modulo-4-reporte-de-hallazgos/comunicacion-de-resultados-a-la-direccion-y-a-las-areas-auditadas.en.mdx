---
title: "Communicating Results to Leadership and Audited Areas"
description: "Presenting findings without creating rejection"
---

How audit results are communicated influences their real impact as much as the technical content of the findings, because a defensive or accusatory presentation toward the audited area usually creates rejection and resistance to implementing the fixes, while improvement-oriented communication facilitates collaboration. In practice, it's worth holding a closing meeting with the audited area before issuing the final report, to validate facts, correct misunderstandings and give them room to explain context the auditor may not know, reserving the formal report to senior management for a later stage where the already validated findings are presented along with their risk level. Separating these two audiences prevents leadership from first receiving an unverified version of the facts, and also keeps the audited area from feeling exposed before its superiors without a prior opportunity to give its side.
