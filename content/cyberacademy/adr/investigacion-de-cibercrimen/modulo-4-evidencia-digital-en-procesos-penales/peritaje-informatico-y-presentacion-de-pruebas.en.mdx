---
title: "Digital Forensics Expert Testimony and Presentation of Evidence"
description: "Turning technical findings into judicial proof"
---

In a criminal proceeding for a computer crime, the technical evidence collected only has value if an expert can present it to the court in a way that meets the required standards of proof, which means translating complex findings, such as server logs or malware analysis, into language that judges and lawyers without technical training can understand and critically evaluate. Digital forensics expert work in this context requires not only technical mastery but knowledge of local procedural standards on what constitutes a valid expert opinion, how the expert must be appointed under the applicable legislation, and what formalities the report must meet to be admitted as evidence. In practice, a well-built expert report explicitly connects each technical finding with the element of the offense it seeks to prove, avoiding presenting technical data that's interesting but irrelevant to the facts actually being tried, a common mistake that dilutes the impact of the evidence and makes the case less clear before the court.
