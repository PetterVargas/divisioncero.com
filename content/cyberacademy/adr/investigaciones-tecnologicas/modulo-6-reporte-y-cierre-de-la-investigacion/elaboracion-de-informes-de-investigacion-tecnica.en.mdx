---
title: "Writing Technical Investigation Reports"
description: "How to write a solid forensic report"
---

The technical investigation report is the final product that turns months of analysis into a document that must hold up to legal, executive or audit review, so its structure and precision are as important as the quality of the technical work behind it. A good report clearly presents the scope, the methodology used, the evidence's chain of custody, the findings backed by concrete data and the conclusions, always separating proven facts from the investigator's hypotheses or inferences. In practice, the most common mistake is mixing opinion with evidence, which weakens the credibility of the whole document before a lawyer or auditor who questions any unsupported statement. Including technical appendices with screenshots, file hashes and detailed timelines, while keeping the main body readable for non-technical readers, is the practice that lets the same report serve both a court and the board of directors.
