---
title: "Evaluating the Effectiveness of the Threat Intelligence Program"
description: "Measuring whether intelligence adds real value"
---

Evaluating the effectiveness of a threat intelligence program means measuring with concrete data whether the investment in sources, tools and analysts really improves the organization's detection and decision-making capability, instead of assuming that more intelligence always means more security. In practice, this requires metrics such as the percentage of indicators that actually become active detections, the time between the publication of a relevant threat and its application in controls, and whether the reports produced actually influence leadership decisions or are simply filed unread. A program can have access to dozens of expensive feeds and still be ineffective if nobody filters the information relevant to the organization's specific context, generating noise instead of actionable value. The key nuance is that effectiveness isn't measured by the volume of data processed, but by concrete decisions that changed thanks to that intelligence, such as a control implemented before an emerging threat actually affected the organization.
