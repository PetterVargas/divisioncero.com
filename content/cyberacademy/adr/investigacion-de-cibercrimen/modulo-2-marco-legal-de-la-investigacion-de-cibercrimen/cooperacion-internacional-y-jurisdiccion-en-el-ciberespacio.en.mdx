---
title: "International Cooperation and Jurisdiction in Cyberspace"
description: "When crime crosses borders the law doesn't"
---

An attack can originate in one country, pass through intermediate servers in three others and affect victims in a fifth, while each jurisdiction involved has its own laws, deadlines and cooperation requirements, which makes jurisdiction one of the biggest practical obstacles in cybercrime investigation. Instruments such as the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime seek to standardize cooperation between signatory countries, speeding up requests for evidence preservation and extradition, but their reach is limited because not all relevant countries have ratified it, and some that have rarely cooperate in practice when the suspect operates under state protection. In practice, investigators depend on formal channels such as mutual legal assistance requests, which can take months to resolve, during which time relevant digital evidence may disappear because of the service providers' own retention policies, which forces urgent preservation requests to be prioritized ahead of the full formal request.
