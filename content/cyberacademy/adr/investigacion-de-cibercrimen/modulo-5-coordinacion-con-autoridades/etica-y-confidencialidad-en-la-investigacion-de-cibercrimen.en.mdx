---
title: "Ethics and Confidentiality in Cybercrime Investigation"
description: "The ethical limits an investigator must not cross"
---

Investigating cybercrime frequently involves accessing extremely sensitive information, from victims' personal data to the private communications of possible suspects, which places ethical responsibilities on the investigator that go beyond strictly legal compliance. Keeping the information collected confidential, limiting access to that data to those who really need it for the case, and avoiding leaks that could alert a suspect or unnecessarily expose a victim are principles that, if violated, can compromise the entire investigation as well as create legal liability for whoever breaches them. In practice, situations such as finding evidence of a crime other than the one originally being investigated, or discovering personal information irrelevant to the case during a forensic analysis, test the investigator's ethical judgment, who must decide what to report and what to keep out of the case file without compromising the integrity of the investigation or exceeding the legally authorized scope for acting on that data.
