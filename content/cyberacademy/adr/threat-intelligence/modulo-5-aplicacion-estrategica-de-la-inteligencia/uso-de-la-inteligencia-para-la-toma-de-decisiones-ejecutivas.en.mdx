---
title: "Using Intelligence for Executive Decision-Making"
description: "Turning intelligence into business decisions"
---

Using threat intelligence to support executive decisions means translating technical findings into clear business implications, such as financial, reputational or regulatory risk, so leadership can decide on investments, priorities or risk tolerance with real information instead of general perceptions about cybersecurity. In practice, this requires the intelligence team to understand the organization's strategic priorities, in order to connect an emerging threat trend with a specific business project that could be affected, instead of presenting generic reports on the global threat landscape that don't translate into any concrete decision. A practical example is presenting the board with evidence that a competitor in the same sector suffered a ransomware attack through a specific vector, which justifies an investment in targeted controls far more convincingly than an abstract statement about the general rise of ransomware in the industry. Well-communicated intelligence turns cybersecurity from a hard-to-justify expense into an informed risk decision.
