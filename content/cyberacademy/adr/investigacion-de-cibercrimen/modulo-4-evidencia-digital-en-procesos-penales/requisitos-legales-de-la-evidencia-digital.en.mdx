---
title: "Legal Requirements for Digital Evidence"
description: "What the law requires to accept digital proof"
---

Not all technically valid digital evidence automatically meets the legal requirements to be accepted in a criminal proceeding; besides demonstrable integrity, the law requires that obtaining that evidence respected procedural guarantees such as due process and, in many cases, prior judicial authorization to access private communications or data stored by third parties. In practice, this means an investigator may have in hand the exact record proving a crime, but if it was obtained without the corresponding legal authorization, that evidence may be excluded from the proceeding and may even legally compromise the investigator who obtained it irregularly. The requirements also include complete traceability of the data's origin, the ability to verify its authenticity through metadata or digital signatures, and the absence of manipulation after its collection. Knowing these requirements before starting any action is what separates an investigation that sustains a case in court from one that ruins it through an avoidable procedural defect.
