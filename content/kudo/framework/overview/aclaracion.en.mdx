<Callout type="info" title="Clarification">
Kudo is a cybersecurity framework **created from critical analysis and practical experience accumulated in real business contexts in Latin America**.
</Callout>

## How was Kudo born?

Kudo comes from our [philosophy](/en/blog) and from a concrete question: *Why are existing cybersecurity frameworks hard to adopt in Latin American companies?*

The answer isn't that global standards are wrong. The answer is that **context matters**. Company size, team maturity, available budget, the company's risk appetite, the region's regulatory reality and the growth speed of tech companies in LatAm create needs that the big frameworks address with a very high barrier to entry.

Kudo was built from scratch applying the following process:

1. **Direct observation** of how cybersecurity teams operate in companies in the region.
2. **Analysis of the most frequent problems** organizations face when trying to implement security controls.
3. **Our own synthesis** of which controls are truly actionable, measurable and sustainable over time.
4. **Iterative validation** through professional practice across different industries and company sizes.

The result is a **structure of 8 domains and their associated controls**, whose grouping, naming, definition and approach are original.

## Use of reference frameworks

Kudo includes a [Mapping](/en/kudo/sgx/correlacion) section based on our ISMS that maps its controls to recognized standards such as ISO 27001:2022, NIST CSF, CIS Controls, SOC 2, PCI DSS, among others.

This mapping has a clear purpose: **making it easier for organizations already working with those standards to adopt Kudo**, or vice versa. The mapping is a traceability reference to the tools we keep building.

## Framework license and use

<Callout title="Open Source">
Kudo is an open-source project. Its source code is available on [GitHub](https://github.com/PetterVargas/divisioncero.com/tree/main/content/kudo). The framework can be freely used, adapted and referenced, as long as its original authorship is acknowledged.
</Callout>

<Accordions type="single">
  <Accordion title="Can I use Kudo in my company?">
    Yes. Kudo is designed to be adopted by any organization. You can implement its domains and controls as an internal reference framework without restrictions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I modify or adapt the framework?">
    Yes. The repository is publicly available. If you make significant modifications, we recommend noting that it's a derived version to avoid confusion with the original framework.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Kudo replace an ISO or NIST certification?">
    No. Kudo doesn't grant certifications and isn't equivalent to a formal audit. It's a practical implementation framework. To obtain recognized certifications, organizations must follow each standard's formal processes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is there any legal risk in using Kudo together with ISO 27001 or NIST?">
    No. Referencing, comparing or mapping frameworks is standard practice in the cybersecurity industry. Kudo doesn't reproduce protected content from any standard.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
