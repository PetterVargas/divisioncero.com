---
title: "Difference between Point-in-Time and Continuous Compliance"
description: "From the annual snapshot to constant monitoring"
---

Point-in-time compliance is the traditional approach in which an organization gathers evidence and fixes gaps just before an audit or certification, achieving compliance at that specific moment but with no guarantee that it remains valid for the rest of the year, while continuous compliance integrates control verification as a constant part of operations, detecting and correcting deviations almost in real time. The practical difference is enormous in the face of a security incident, because an organization with point-in-time compliance may discover, already in the middle of the crisis, that a control certified six months ago stopped working weeks earlier without anyone noticing, while a continuous compliance program would have raised an alert much sooner. Moving from one model to the other doesn't require discarding periodic audits, but complementing them with automated monitoring and frequent reviews that shrink the window of time in which a control can fail without being detected.
