---
title: "Reporting Protocols for Competent Authorities"
description: "Reporting well also protects the organization"
---

Reporting an incident to the competent authorities isn't just a legal requirement in many jurisdictions, but a strategic decision that, made with a clear protocol, protects both the victim and the future investigation of the case. A well-designed protocol defines in advance which specific authority to report to depending on the type of crime, what minimum information the initial report must include, who within the organization has the authority to file it and within what time frame, considering that some regulations impose notification windows of only 72 hours after detecting the incident. In practice, many organizations improvise this process during the crisis itself, which leads to incomplete reports, delays that may constitute an additional regulatory violation, or the loss of evidence that should have been preserved before notifying. Having protocols already defined and tested through drills, with up-to-date templates and contacts for each relevant authority, significantly reduces response time when a real incident occurs.
