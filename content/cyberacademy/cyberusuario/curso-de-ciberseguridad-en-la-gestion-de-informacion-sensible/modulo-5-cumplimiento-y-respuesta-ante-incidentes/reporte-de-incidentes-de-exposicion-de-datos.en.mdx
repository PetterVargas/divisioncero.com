---
title: "Reporting Data Exposure Incidents"
description: "How and when to speak up if information was exposed"
---

Noticing that you sent sensitive information to the wrong person, that you left a confidential document visible on a shared printer, or that a file with customer data was publicly accessible by mistake triggers a natural reaction to try to fix it quietly before anyone else finds out, but that reaction usually makes things worse. Reporting the incident immediately, even if it seems minor or you think you've already solved it yourself, gives the organization the chance to contain the damage, notify in time if the law requires it and keep the problem from getting worse while nobody else knows. Security teams almost always prefer to receive ten reports of minor incidents that turned out not to be serious than to find out late about a serious one that could have been contained in time. Knowing in advance the right channel for reporting this type of incident, and understanding that doing so isn't about blaming anyone but protecting everyone, is what really makes the difference when something goes wrong with sensitive information.
