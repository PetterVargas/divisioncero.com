---
title: "The PASTA Methodology and Its Risk-Oriented Approach"
description: "Threat modeling centered on business risk"
---

PASTA, which stands for Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis, is a seven-stage methodology that differs from approaches such as STRIDE through its strong orientation toward business risk, explicitly seeking to connect each identified technical threat with its real impact on the organization's objectives, not just to catalog attack types in an abstract way disconnected from context. The process starts by defining the relevant business objectives and compliance requirements, continues with technical analysis of the architecture, moves on to decomposing the application and identifying concrete threats and vulnerabilities, and culminates in an analysis of simulated attacks and the calculation of residual impact on the business, which makes it possible to prioritize with financial and operational criteria, not just technical ones. This depth makes PASTA especially valuable for complex, high-risk systems, where justifying investment in security controls against business decisions requires talking in terms of economic and reputational impact, not just the technical severity of a vulnerability. Its main cost is the time and coordination it requires between technical and business teams, so it's usually reserved for critical systems instead of being applied routinely to every minor product change.
