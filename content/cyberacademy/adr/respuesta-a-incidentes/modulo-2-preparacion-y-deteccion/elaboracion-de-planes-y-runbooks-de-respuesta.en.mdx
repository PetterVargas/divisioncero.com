---
title: "Building Response Plans and Runbooks"
description: "Creating step-by-step action guides"
---

A response plan defines the organization's overall strategy for an incident, while runbooks are detailed step-by-step guides for specific scenarios, such as ransomware, account compromise or data exfiltration, and both must exist before the crisis happens, not be improvised during it. In practice, an effective runbook specifies exactly which commands to run, which tools to use and whom to escalate to at each decision point, reducing the cognitive load on the analyst who's under pressure with the clock ticking. The most common mistake when creating these documents is making them too generic or letting them fall out of date as the infrastructure changes, so that when they're finally needed they refer to systems that no longer exist or steps that no longer apply. Testing runbooks through periodic drills, instead of leaving them as theoretical documents, is what reveals their gaps before a real incident exposes them at the worst possible moment.
