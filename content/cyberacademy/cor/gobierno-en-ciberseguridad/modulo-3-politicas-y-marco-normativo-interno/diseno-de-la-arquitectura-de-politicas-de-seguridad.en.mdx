---
title: "Designing the Security Policy Architecture"
description: "Organizing the hierarchy of internal rules"
---

The security policy architecture is the hierarchical structure that organizes an organization's various normative documents, typically in layers that range from the general security policy, approved by leadership and high-level, to standards that set specific technical requirements, procedures that detail concrete operational steps, and guidelines that offer non-mandatory recommendations. Designing this architecture correctly avoids two common problems: a single monolithic document that mixes general principles with technical details and becomes hard to keep up to date, or a scattered set of documents with no clear relationship to each other that ends up being contradictory. In practice, each level of the architecture should have a different owner and review frequency, because the general policy can remain stable for years while technical procedures must be updated as often as the underlying technology evolves, and confusing these two update rhythms produces outdated documents or unnecessary reviews.
