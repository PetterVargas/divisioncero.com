---
title: "Preserving Evidence for Judicial Proceedings"
description: "Acting fast before evidence disappears"
---

Digital evidence relevant to a criminal proceeding, such as connection logs, emails or online account metadata, usually has a limited lifespan, because service providers delete records according to their own retention policies, which are sometimes days rather than months. Preserving this evidence in time requires acting quickly, typically through formal preservation requests that instruct the provider to freeze existing data without deleting it, even before obtaining a full court order authorizing its disclosure, a mechanism that exists precisely because full legal processes usually take longer than standard data retention lasts. In practice, many investigations fail not because the evidence didn't exist, but because nobody requested its preservation within the period the provider kept it, a mistake that usually stems from the lack of agile coordination between the victim, law enforcement and legal teams during the first critical hours after detecting an incident.
