---
title: "Governance Structures: Committees and Key Roles"
description: "Bodies and roles that sustain governance"
---

A cybersecurity governance program rests on formal structures that distribute decision-making, including a risk and security committee that periodically reviews the state of the program and approves wider-reaching decisions, a crisis committee that is activated in the event of serious incidents, and key roles such as the CISO, who leads the security strategy, and, in larger organizations, a risk or compliance officer who provides an independent view. Defining these structures clearly prevents security decisions from being informally concentrated in a single person, which creates excessive dependence and a lack of continuity if that person leaves the organization. In practice, the effectiveness of these structures depends less on their formal existence on an org chart and more on whether they actually meet at the defined frequency, have real authority to make binding decisions, and have quality information to discuss, because a committee that only approves what had already been decided provides no real governance.
