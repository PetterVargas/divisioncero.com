---
title: "Presenting Findings to Non-Technical and Legal Audiences"
description: "Communicating findings to executives and lawyers"
---

Presenting technical findings to executives, lawyers or authorities requires translating complex concepts into language those audiences can use to make decisions, without losing the precision the evidence requires. In practice, this means avoiding unnecessary jargon, relying on clear analogies and structuring the presentation around the questions that really matter to the audience, such as what happened, what impact it had, who was responsible and what should be done now, instead of listing every technical detail of the analysis. Legal teams in particular need conclusions to be clearly backed by traceable evidence, because any ambiguous statement can weaken a possible legal action or a defense before a regulator. A common mistake is overloading the presentation with technical screenshots the audience can't interpret; it's more effective to summarize the finding in one sentence and leave the technical detail available as an appendix for whoever needs to review it.
