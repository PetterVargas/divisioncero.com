---
title: "Chain of Custody in the Acquisition Process"
description: "Documenting every hand the evidence passed through"
---

The chain of custody is the documented record of every person who came into contact with the digital evidence, from the moment of its acquisition to its final presentation in a legal proceeding, including dates, locations, storage conditions and the purpose of each handling. Its practical importance is enormous because a single unexplained break, for example a hard drive that was outside documented custody for several hours, can be enough for opposing counsel to question the integrity of all the evidence and have it excluded from the case, no matter how solid the subsequent technical analysis is. In practice, maintaining a rigorous chain of custody means using standardized forms, tamper-evident security seals on physical devices and case management systems that automatically record every access, because relying on incomplete manual records is one of the most common causes of digital evidence being thrown out at trial.
