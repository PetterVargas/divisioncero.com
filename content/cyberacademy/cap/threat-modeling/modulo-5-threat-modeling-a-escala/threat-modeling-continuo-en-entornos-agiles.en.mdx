---
title: "Continuous Threat Modeling in Agile Environments"
description: "Adapting threat modeling to short sprints"
---

Traditional threat modeling, conceived as an extensive, exhaustive exercise carried out once at the start of a project, fits poorly with teams that work in short sprints and deploy changes several times a week, because by the time a complete analysis is finished, the system has already evolved significantly. Adapting it to agile environments requires breaking the process into small, focused sessions tied directly to the concrete changes each sprint introduces, instead of trying to review the whole system each time; if a user story adds a new external integration, the corresponding threat modeling session focuses specifically on that integration and its new data flows, not on repeating the full analysis of the system. This incremental approach requires maintaining a living, cumulative threat model that's updated with each iteration, instead of generating a new, disconnected document in each session, so the team builds an increasingly complete understanding of the system instead of constantly starting from scratch. For this to work sustainably, it's worth training developers themselves to recognize when a change warrants a threat modeling session, instead of relying exclusively on an external specialist who becomes the process bottleneck.
