---
title: "Threat Modeling"
description: "Anticipating threats before programming"
---

Threat modeling consists of systematically identifying what can go wrong in an application before writing the first line of code, instead of discovering it later through a real incident. A widely used approach is STRIDE, which evaluates each system component against six threat categories: spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service and elevation of privilege. The result isn't a document that gets filed away, but a concrete list of mitigations built directly into the design: which data to encrypt, which validations to add, which permissions to restrict. Doing it early in the design, and not as a later audit, makes it possible to correct architectural decisions that would be much costlier to change once the system is already in production.
