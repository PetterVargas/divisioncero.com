---
title: "Principles of Forensic Science Applied to the Digital Realm"
description: "The foundations that support all forensic analysis"
---

Digital forensics isn't simply using technical tools to look for suspicious files; it inherits principles from traditional forensic science that ensure its conclusions can hold up in court. Notable among them is Locard's exchange principle, which states that every contact leaves a trace, applied in the digital realm to the idea that any interaction with a system, even the analysis itself, can modify evidence if it isn't done with extreme care. Also central are the expert's objectivity, which requires reporting findings without bias toward any of the parties involved, and reproducibility, meaning that another independent expert following the same documented process reaches equivalent conclusions. In practice, ignoring these principles turns a technically correct analysis into questionable evidence, because the opposing legal party can attack not the finding itself but the process followed to obtain it, and that distinction decides many court cases.
