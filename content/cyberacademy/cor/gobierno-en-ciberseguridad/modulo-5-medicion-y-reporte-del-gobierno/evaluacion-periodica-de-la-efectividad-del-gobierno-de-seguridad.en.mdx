---
title: "Periodic Evaluation of Security Governance Effectiveness"
description: "Auditing whether governance really governs"
---

Periodically evaluating the effectiveness of security governance itself means asking whether the defined structures, committees and roles are fulfilling their real function, beyond formally existing in a governance document, reviewing whether decisions that should be made at the leadership level are actually made there, or whether in practice they end up being resolved informally at a lower technical level without the visibility the original design intended. This evaluation can rely on interviews with committee members, a review of minutes to verify the real quality of the discussions versus automatic approvals, and comparison against governance maturity models recognized in the industry. In practice, a common finding in these evaluations is that governance works well in documented theory but dilutes under operational pressure, for example when a risk decision that should have been escalated to the committee is resolved directly at the technical level because the formal process is perceived as too slow for the urgency of the moment.
