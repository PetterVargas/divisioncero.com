---
title: "Notifying Authorities and Affected Parties"
description: "Meeting legal notification obligations"
---

Notifying regulatory authorities and affected parties after an incident involving personal data or critical infrastructure isn't optional in most jurisdictions, and the deadlines are usually strict, often counted in hours from when the breach is confirmed and not from when the initial suspicion is detected. In practice, this requires legal and security to work together from the start of the response, because deciding what to notify, to whom and with what level of detail requires both technical judgment about the real scope of the compromise and specific regulatory knowledge of the sector and region. A nuance that's often overlooked is that over-notifying, with information not yet confirmed, can be as damaging as notifying late, since it leads to public retractions that erode trust more than a careful initial communication that's progressively updated. Preparing notification templates in advance, before the incident happens, significantly reduces response time when regulatory pressure is already on.
