---
title: "Building Remediation Plans with Owners and Deadlines"
description: "Turning findings into concrete actions"
---

A finding without an associated remediation plan is rarely fixed, because it remains an abstract observation with nobody taking responsibility for resolving it and no date that makes it enforceable. Building a good plan means defining the specific action to be taken, who the formal owner of that action is within the organization, and a realistic deadline agreed with that person rather than imposed unilaterally by the auditor. In practice, the most effective plans distinguish between an immediate fix that mitigates the risk while the definitive solution is implemented, and the underlying remediation that addresses the root cause, since treating them as a single action usually leads to unrealistic deadlines or premature closures. It's also worth including an automatic escalation mechanism when a deadline is missed, so the delay reaches leadership before it becomes a recurring finding in the next audit.
