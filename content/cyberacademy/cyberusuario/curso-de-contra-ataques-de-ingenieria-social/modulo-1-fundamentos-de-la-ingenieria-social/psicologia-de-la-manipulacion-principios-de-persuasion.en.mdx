---
title: "The Psychology of Manipulation: Principles of Persuasion"
description: "Psychological principles attackers use"
---

Attackers rely on principles of persuasion well documented by social psychology. Authority, posing as a boss, vendor or official body to reduce questioning. Urgency, forcing quick decisions before the victim can calmly verify the request. Reciprocity, offering something first (a favor, a gift) to create a sense of debt. Social proof, suggesting that "others already did it" to normalize the requested action. And scarcity, hinting that an opportunity will disappear if they don't act immediately. None of these principles is bad in itself, since they're also used in legitimate marketing, but combined with a false identity they become tools of manipulation. Recognizing them in the moment is the first step to resisting them.
