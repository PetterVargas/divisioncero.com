---
title: "Right to Audit Critical Vendors"
description: "Why include the right to audit in contracts"
---

The right to audit is a contractual clause that allows the organization, or an independent third party on its behalf, to directly verify that a vendor meets the agreed security controls, instead of relying only on its word or self-declared certifications. Without this clause, the organization is left blindly trusting the security of a vendor that handles critical data or systems, with no real ability to verify it when doubts arise. Negotiating this right before signing the contract is key, because afterward it's much harder to impose, and it's worth also including the right to require evidence of remediation when the audit reveals gaps. In practice, many vendors resist full audits for reasons of confidentiality with other customers, so it's common to negotiate equivalent alternatives, such as independent third-party audit reports like SOC 2, which offer reasonable verification without compromising the information of the vendor's other customers.
