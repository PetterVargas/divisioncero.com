---
title: "Risks of Fake or Modified Apps"
description: "How to identify cloned or altered apps"
---

Fake apps imitate popular brands such as banks, social networks or streaming services, using nearly identical logos and names to fool people who search quickly in the store or click a shared link. Their goal is usually to steal credentials, show invasive advertising or install malicious software disguised as a legitimate feature. Modified versions of real apps are just as dangerous, because they promise extra features or ad removal in exchange for installing the file from outside the official store, and that's precisely where you lose the security guarantees the normal process offers. To avoid them, it's worth always checking the developer's exact name, comparing the number of downloads with what you'd expect for a well-known brand and being wary of any app that asks you to enable unusual permissions as soon as it opens. When something feels exaggerated or too good, such as a banking app with features the real bank doesn't offer, it's almost always a warning sign.
