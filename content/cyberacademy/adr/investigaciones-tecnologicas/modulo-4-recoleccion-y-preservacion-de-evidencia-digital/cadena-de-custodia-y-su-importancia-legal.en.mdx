---
title: "Chain of Custody and Its Legal Importance"
description: "Legal traceability of digital evidence"
---

The chain of custody is the documented record of who had access to a piece of digital evidence, when, where and what they did with it from the moment it was collected until its final presentation, and it's what makes it possible to show that the evidence wasn't altered or tampered with along the way. Without this record, even the most compelling technical evidence can be thrown out of a legal proceeding because there's no way to guarantee its integrity before a judge or opposing counsel. In practice, maintaining the chain of custody means documenting cryptographic hashes at the time of acquisition, recording each physical or logical transfer of the device or file, and limiting access to authorized staff only, with their respective signature or digital record. A common case where this fails is when an analyst copies a file to their own machine to review it "quickly" without documenting it, breaking traceability and creating reasonable doubt that can invalidate months of investigative work.
