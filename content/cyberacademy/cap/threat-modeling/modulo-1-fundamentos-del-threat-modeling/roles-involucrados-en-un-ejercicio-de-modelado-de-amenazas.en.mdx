---
title: "Roles Involved in a Threat Modeling Exercise"
description: "Who should take part for a complete analysis"
---

A threat modeling exercise carried out exclusively by the security team, without the participation of those who designed and deeply know the system, tends to produce a superficial analysis that sticks to generic textbook threats instead of reflecting the real, specific risks of that particular architecture. The architects and developers who build the system bring detailed technical knowledge of how it really works, information that's rarely fully documented and that's essential for identifying plausible threats; the security specialist brings the methodological framework and experience with attack patterns the development team may not know firsthand. When the system handles sensitive data or has regulatory implications, it's also worth including someone with a business or compliance perspective, able to translate a technical finding into its real impact on the organization and help prioritize with that criterion. The session facilitator plays a different but equally important role: keeping the conversation focused, ensuring all relevant threat categories are systematically covered according to the chosen methodology, and preventing the discussion from getting stuck debating a single scenario while others go unanalyzed for lack of time.
