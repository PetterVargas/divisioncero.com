---
title: "Updating Plans and Controls Based on Lessons Learned"
description: "Turning lessons learned into improvements"
---

Updating plans and controls after an incident is what turns a lesson learned into a real improvement, and not just a filed note nobody consults again. In practice, this means reviewing the response plan, the specific runbooks and the affected technical controls in light of what actually happened, adjusting steps that turned out to be ambiguous, adding missing detections or correcting permissions that allowed the attacker's lateral movement. A frequent mistake is treating the update as a low-value administrative task, when in reality it requires assigning owners, deadlines and follow-up like any other critical security project. For example, if the post-mortem revealed that the team took hours to identify which systems were affected because there was no up-to-date asset inventory, the concrete improvement isn't "improve visibility" but implementing and maintaining that inventory, with a deadline and a clearly assigned owner, so the next response is faster.
