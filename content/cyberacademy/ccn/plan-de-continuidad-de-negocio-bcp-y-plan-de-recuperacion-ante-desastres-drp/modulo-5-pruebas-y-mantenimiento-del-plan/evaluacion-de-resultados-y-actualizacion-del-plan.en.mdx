---
title: "Evaluating Results and Updating the Plan"
description: "Closing the loop with test-based improvements"
---

Every exercise, drill or real activation of the continuity plan generates valuable information that's lost if it isn't evaluated in a structured way: how long each step really took compared with the planned time, which procedures were confusing, which dependencies weren't documented. Evaluating these results means producing an honest gap report, not a report that downplays failures to look good to leadership, and translating those gaps into concrete changes to the plan, not just filed observations. This matters because a continuity plan isn't a static document but a living process that must improve with each test cycle; otherwise the same mistakes are repeated in every successive drill. A practical example is updating a system's RTO if the drill consistently shows that the real recovery takes twice as long as originally estimated on paper.
