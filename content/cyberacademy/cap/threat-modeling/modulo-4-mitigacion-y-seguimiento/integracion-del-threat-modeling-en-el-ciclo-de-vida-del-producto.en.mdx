---
title: "Integrating Threat Modeling into the Product Lifecycle"
description: "Inserting threat modeling into every phase"
---

Threat modeling provides the most value when it's integrated at specific, predictable moments of the product lifecycle, not when it's run in isolation and sporadically with no connection to the rest of the development process. The natural entry point is the design phase of a significant new feature, before any code is written, because that's when the architecture changes resulting from identifying a critical threat are still cheap to implement. Beyond that first moment, it's worth defining clear triggers that require reviewing or updating the existing model, such as adding a new external component, a significant change in sensitive data flows, or exposing previously internal functionality to external users. A pattern that distinguishes mature organizations is not treating threat modeling as a one-time event per project, but as a living artifact revisited in periodic architecture reviews, so the model faithfully reflects the real system and not an outdated snapshot of how the product was originally designed months or years ago.
