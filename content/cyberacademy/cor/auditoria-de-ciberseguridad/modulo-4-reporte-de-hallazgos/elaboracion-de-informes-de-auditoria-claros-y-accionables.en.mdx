---
title: "Writing Clear, Actionable Audit Reports"
description: "Writing so the reader acts, not just reads"
---

A technically correct but hard-to-read audit report ends up filed away without generating any action, so writing it clearly and actionably is as important as the quality of the fieldwork behind it. This means adapting the language to the audience that will receive it, including an executive summary without technical jargon for senior management, and reserving the full technical detail for appendices aimed at the operational teams who must implement the remediation. Each finding should be presented following a consistent structure, with the risk expressed in business terms and not just technical ones, and a concrete recommendation the responsible person can carry out without further interpretation. In practice, a good report avoids vague phrases such as improve access controls and instead specifies the exact action expected, for example implementing multi-factor authentication on the critical systems identified in finding three.
