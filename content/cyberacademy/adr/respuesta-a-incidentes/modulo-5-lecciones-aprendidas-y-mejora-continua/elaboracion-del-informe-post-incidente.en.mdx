---
title: "Writing the Post-Incident Report"
description: "Documenting the incident to improve processes"
---

The post-incident report documents in a structured way what happened, how the team responded, what worked and what didn't, with the goal of turning the experience into organizational learning instead of leaving it as an anecdote forgotten within weeks. A good report includes the complete incident timeline, the real impact measured in downtime or affected data, the decisions made at each stage and an honest evaluation of detection, containment and recovery times against the previously defined objectives. In practice, the report's value depends on it being written in a blameless environment, because if the team fears personal consequences for admitting mistakes, the document ends up as a sanitized version that hides the real causes of the problem. A typical example is openly acknowledging that a critical alert was ignored because of alert fatigue, which is uncomfortable to admit but is exactly the kind of finding that prevents the next similar incident.
