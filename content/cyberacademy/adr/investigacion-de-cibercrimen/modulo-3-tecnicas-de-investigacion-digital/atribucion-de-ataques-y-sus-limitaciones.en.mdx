---
title: "Attack Attribution and Its Limitations"
description: "Why pointing to the culprit is so hard"
---

Attributing an attack to a specific actor, whether a criminal group or a state, is one of the most complex and least exact tasks in cybercrime investigation, because advanced attackers deliberately use false flag techniques, reuse other groups' tools and route their attacks through the compromised infrastructure of innocent third parties to throw the investigation off course. Technical attribution relies on evidence such as a group's characteristic tactics and procedures, code similarity across campaigns and infrastructure patterns, but that evidence rarely reaches the level of absolute certainty a criminal proceeding requires, which is why many public attributions are expressed with confidence levels, not as proven facts. In practice, this limitation has real consequences: governments and companies sometimes attribute attacks for political or public relations purposes based on circumstantial evidence, which has led to cases of mistaken attributions that later had to be corrected, damaging the credibility of whoever made them.
