---
title: "Managing the Lifecycle of Security Policies"
description: "Keeping rules alive and current"
---

A security policy's lifecycle doesn't end when it's approved; it requires ongoing management that includes communicating it effectively to the whole organization, verifying that it's being followed in practice, reviewing it periodically to confirm it's still relevant, and eventually updating or retiring it when it no longer applies to the current business context. Without this actively managed lifecycle, it's common to find organizations with policies valid on paper that nobody has reviewed in years, referencing technologies or organizational structures that no longer exist, causing confusion when an auditor or employee tries to apply them literally. In practice, each policy should have a mandatory review date assigned, typically annual, and an owner responsible for starting that process, because leaving updates to the spontaneous initiative of someone on the security team is the surest way for that process to simply never happen.
