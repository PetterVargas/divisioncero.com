---
title: "The Threat Intelligence Lifecycle"
description: "The phases of the intelligence process"
---

The threat intelligence lifecycle describes the continuous process that turns raw data into actionable intelligence, going through planning and direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination and feedback, and understanding this flow keeps a program from getting stuck accumulating data without producing real value. In practice, the planning phase is the most decisive because it defines which intelligence questions really matter to the organization, preventing analysts from collecting information that's interesting but irrelevant to the specific threats the business faces. Feedback closes the cycle and is usually the most forgotten phase, although it's the one that makes it possible to adjust collection priorities according to which intelligence actually proved useful in past decisions. A practical example of why this cycle matters is when a team collects hundreds of generic indicators without going through a rigorous analysis that puts them in context, handing end consumers a list of data without the interpretation that turns it into useful, actionable intelligence.
