---
title: "Continuous Improvement of the Vendor Management Program"
description: "How to evolve the third-party management program"
---

A third-party risk management program isn't a set of controls implemented once and left fixed; it needs to be reviewed periodically against the incidents that have occurred, lessons learned from previous assessments and the evolution of threats and applicable regulations. Continuous improvement means measuring the effectiveness of the program itself, not just each vendor's risk, for example how long it takes to complete an assessment, what percentage of critical vendors have expired assessments, or how many incidents originated with vendors that had been approved without observations. These indicators reveal whether the program is maturing or simply generating paperwork without reducing real risk. A program that never questions itself tends to become a bureaucratic formality disconnected from real threats, approving vendors through checklists nobody has updated since they were designed.
