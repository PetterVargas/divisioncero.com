---
title: "Justifying the Return on Security Investment"
description: "Making the case for security spending"
---

Justifying the return on security investment is a particular challenge because its main benefit isn't generating revenue but avoiding losses that, if the control works well, never materialize, which makes it hard to present figures as direct as those of a traditional business project. A practical approach is to express value in terms of avoided risk, comparing the cost of the investment with the expected financial loss it reduces, using probability and impact estimates for the scenarios the control mitigates, complemented with references to the real cost of similar incidents in the same industry. In practice, it's also worth arguing the return in terms of operational continuity and business trust, not just loss reduction, because decisions such as certifying the organization under a recognized standard can open up contracts or markets that would otherwise remain closed, a tangible business benefit that strengthens the investment case beyond the purely defensive argument of avoided risk.
