---
title: "Legislation Applicable to Computer Crimes"
description: "The legal framework that defines computer crime"
---

Before investigating any incident as a crime, the conduct must actually be defined as a crime in local legislation, something that varies considerably between countries and that determines what powers the authority has to act, what evidence can legally be requested and what penalties apply. Many national computer crime laws arose as late adaptations of criminal codes designed for physical crimes, which creates obvious gaps in the face of new conduct such as ransomware as a service or deepfake fraud, where the law still doesn't clearly define the responsibility of each actor in the chain. In practice, an investigator must know not only the substantive criminal law but also the procedural rules governing the collection of digital evidence, such as the requirements for requesting a court order to access data stored in the cloud by a foreign provider, because a procedural error at that stage can invalidate all the evidence collected afterward, no matter how technically solid it is.
