---
title: "Lessons Learned from Previous Change Processes"
description: "Turning past changes into continuous improvement"
---

Every change process leaves concrete evidence of what worked and what failed in terms of security, but that information only has value if it's collected systematically and turned into real adjustments to future processes, instead of remaining in the informal memory of those who took part. Documenting lessons learned after a merger, a technology migration or a restructuring makes it possible to identify recurring patterns, such as which types of access tend to go unrevoked or which controls get skipped under time pressure, and to fix them before the next similar project. This matters because organizations that don't close this loop tend to repeat the same security mistakes with each change, even when the people involved change. A practical example is keeping a brief record after each major change, with the minor incidents detected and the corrective actions applied, to consult when planning the next transformation project.
