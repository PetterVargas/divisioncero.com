---
title: "Drafting and Approving Internal Policies and Standards"
description: "Drafting and formalizing security rules"
---

A security policy only carries real weight within the organization when it goes through a formal drafting and approval process that gives it authority, which means writing it in clear, applicable language, submitting it for review by the affected legal and business areas, and obtaining the signature of a leadership level with enough authority to require compliance across the whole organization. Without this formal backing, a policy remains a technical recommendation that any area can ignore without consequences, especially when it conflicts with operational business priorities. In practice, the drafting process should include the teams that will have to comply with the policy from the initial draft, not just at the end for signature, because a policy written in isolation by the security team usually contains unrealistic requirements that generate resistance and constant exceptions once published, undermining its credibility from day one.
