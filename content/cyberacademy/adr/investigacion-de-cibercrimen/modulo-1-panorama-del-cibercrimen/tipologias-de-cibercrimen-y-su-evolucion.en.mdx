---
title: "Cybercrime Typologies and Their Evolution"
description: "How the forms of digital crime have changed"
---

Cybercrime isn't a static category: it has evolved from the basic bank fraud and credit card theft of two decades ago to much more sophisticated models such as double-extortion ransomware, where the attacker not only encrypts systems but also leaks sensitive data and threatens to publish it if the ransom isn't paid, multiplying the pressure on the victim. Classifying the type of crime correctly matters in practice because each typology triggers different legal frameworks, requires different evidence and may involve specific specialized authorities, from financial crime units to data protection prosecutors. Recent evolution also includes AI-assisted fraud, such as cloned voices for phone scams, and software supply chain attacks that compromise thousands of organizations through a single trusted vendor. An investigator who keeps classifying incidents with categories from ten years ago risks applying procedures that no longer respond to the real threat.
